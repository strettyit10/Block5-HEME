LO 1Review: clot formation, control and the lab toolkit

Q1. What are the four steps of hemostasis, and what does the bleeding pattern tell me?
StepWhat happensBreaks →
1 · VasoconstrictionEndothelin, neural reflex—
2 · PrimaryPlatelet plug: vWF–GPIb adhesion, GPIIb/IIIa aggregationMucocutaneous bleeding: petechiae, nosebleeds, gums, heavy periods
3 · SecondaryCascade → thrombin → fibrin meshDeep bleeding: joints, muscles, delayed bleeding after surgery
4 · FibrinolysistPA: plasminogen → plasmin → cleaves fibrin → D-dimer—

Thrombin builds

  • Fibrinogen → fibrin
  • Activates V, VIII, XI, XIII; releases VIII from vWF
  • Activates platelets (PAR-1)

Thrombin + thrombomodulin brakes

  • Activates protein C (+ S) → inactivates Va, VIIIa
  • Endothelium adds PGI₂, NO, heparan sulfate (→ antithrombin), TFPI, tPA
High yield

The bleeding pattern names the step: mucocutaneous = platelets / vWF; deep = factors / inhibitors.

Step 1

Plasmin brakes: PAI-1 (blocks tPA), α2-antiplasmin, TAFI. Tranexamic / aminocaproic acid block plasminogen activation: used for mucosal bleeding, heavy periods and dental work.

Memory tool

PT = "Play Tennis outside" → extrinsic (VII). PTT = "Play Table Tennis inside" → intrinsic (XII, XI, IX, VIII: "$12 → $11.98").

Q3. How do I read a mixing study, and which test comes next?
  • Normal plasma restores a missing factor to ≈50%, which is enough to normalize the test
  • Anti-FVIII antibodies are time- and temperature-dependent: the immediate mix can look corrected, then prolongs after incubation → ask for a mix with prolonged incubation
In-house exam

Mixing rule: corrects = deficiency (lack of a protein); partial or no correction = inhibitor in the patient's plasma.

Analogy — the borrowed cup of sugar

If your recipe failed because you were out of sugar, a neighbor's cup fixes it. If someone keeps eating the sugar, borrowing a cup doesn't help; the thief eats that too.

ApplyLO 1 application questions

A1Day 6 of IV heparin: the aPTT is long, the mix fails, and the thrombin time is very prolonged. Best confirming test?
Anti-Xa level (heparin effect). A very long TT points to heparin.
A2Why does the dRVVT "sure" step shorten in lupus anticoagulant?
Excess phospholipid neutralizes the antiphospholipid antibody, so the clot forms normally.

LO 2Hemophilia B: presentation, differential and confirmation

Q1. How does hemophilia B present?
  • Christmas disease: X-linked recessive deficiency or dysfunction of factor IX (vitamin K–dependent serine protease)
  • Boys; carrier mother, affected maternal uncles (≈⅓ new mutations → family history can be negative)
  • Deep bleeds: hemarthroses, muscle hematomas, intracranial hemorrhage; delayed bleeding after circumcision, dental work or surgery
  • Clinically identical to hemophilia A; no petechiae

Hemarthrosis

  • The classic hemophilia bleed

Muscle / soft-tissue bleed

  • Large hematoma after minor trauma

X-linked recessive

  • Carrier mother → 50% of sons affected
Memory tool

"The boy with the swollen knee." A = 8, B = 9 (B for "Boxing Day": Christmas).

Q2. What else could cause this picture?

Trap

Only a factor assay separates A from B. And newborns normally have low vitamin K–dependent factors (incl. IX), so a borderline FIX in an infant is repeated at ≈6 months.

In-house exam

Exam note: a carrier's factor levels "most resemble" those of the affected relative only if she is extremely lyonized; most carriers have ≈50% and don't bleed.

ApplyLO 2 application questions

A1A 2-year-old boy has a swollen knee after a minor fall; aPTT 75 s, corrected by mixing; PT and platelets normal. Next test?
FVIII and FIX activity to tell hemophilia A from B and set severity.

LO 3Hemophilia severity, treatment and complications

Q1. How does bleeding differ between mild and severe hemophilia?
SeverityFactor activityBleeding (A and B alike)
Severe<1%Spontaneous joint and muscle bleeds from infancy / crawling
Moderate1–5%With minor trauma; occasionally spontaneous
Mild>5–40%Only with surgery or major trauma (may be found as an adult)

Severity by factor level

  • First joint bleed in severe disease ≈18 months
High yield

Severity = factor level, not the type. Severe A and severe B look the same.

Q2. How is severe hemophilia B treated, and why?
  • Prophylactic FIX concentrate (recombinant, extended half-life) → keeps trough levels above ≈1%, turning "severe" into "moderate" and preventing joint damage
  • On-demand FIX for acute bleeds; antifibrinolytics (tranexamic acid) for mucosal or dental bleeding
  • Gene therapy (AAV-delivered F9) for selected adults
  • Avoid IM injections, aspirin, NSAIDs
  • Desmopressin does nothing in B: it releases stored vWF + FVIII, not FIX
Trap

DDAVP works only for mild hemophilia A and type 1 vWD. Never in hemophilia B, and avoid it in vWD type 2B.

Step 1

Emicizumab (bridges IXa and X) is for hemophilia A, with or without inhibitors.

Q3. What are the complications of the disease and of treatment?

Disease

  • Hemophilic arthropathy ("target joints")
  • Muscle bleeds: iliopsoas → femoral nerve palsy; compartment syndrome
  • Intracranial hemorrhage (leading cause of death)

Treatment

  • Inhibitors (alloantibodies): ≈25–30% severe A, 3–5% B
  • In B: anaphylaxis + nephrotic syndrome with FIX in inhibitor patients
  • Thrombosis with bypassing agents
  • Older plasma products → HIV, HCV

ApplyLO 3 application questions

A1A boy with severe hemophilia B on FIX prophylaxis suddenly stops responding, and the aPTT no longer corrects on mixing. What happened, and which test?
He developed an alloantibody inhibitor to FIX → Bethesda assay; switch to bypassing agents.
A2Why won't desmopressin help a patient with mild hemophilia B?
DDAVP releases vWF and FVIII from endothelial Weibel-Palade bodies; it doesn't raise FIX.

LO 5ADAMTS13 and TTP treatment

Q1. How does losing ADAMTS13 cause TTP?
  • ADAMTS13 = metalloprotease that keeps vWF multimers at a normal size
  • Hereditary form (Upshaw-Schulman) = inherited ADAMTS13 deficiency in children
  • Diagnose by ADAMTS13 activity and antigen: activity must be <10%
  • PT and aPTT stay normal: the cascade isn't consumed (unlike DIC)
Analogy — the hedge trimmer

ADAMTS13 is the hedge trimmer for vWF. Without it, the vWF strands grow into a bramble that snags every platelet floating by, and red cells get shredded pushing through.

Q2. How is TTP treated?
  • Plasma exchange: removes the antibody and ultra-large vWF, replaces ADAMTS13
  • Plus immunosuppression: steroids ± rituximab
  • Start immediately on clinical suspicion; don't wait for the ADAMTS13 result
  • Avoid platelet transfusion (feeds the microthrombi)
High yield

TTP = emergency: recognize the signs, start plasma exchange + immunosuppression. Untreated mortality is ≈90%.

Step 1

Caplacizumab (anti-vWF nanobody) blocks vWF–GPIb binding and is added to plasma exchange.

ApplyLO 5 application question

A1A 45-year-old woman with confusion, fever, platelets 28,000, schistocytes and creatinine 3.1. Underlying cause?
Antibodies against ADAMTS13 (acquired TTP).

Memory tool

Bleeds deep = hemophilia · mucocutaneous = vWD · surgical = XI · none = XII · clots = lupus anticoagulant · hospital line = heparin · grandpa with new bruises = acquired inhibitor.

Trap

A prolonged PTT with no bleeding = lupus anticoagulant or FXII deficiency. Neither needs bleeding precautions before surgery.

Q2. What makes acquired hemophilia A so dangerous?
  • Autoantibodies neutralize FVIII in people with no hemophilia: 1–4 per million per year
  • Older adults, postpartum women, autoimmune disease, cancer, drugs; about half idiopathic
  • Severe bleeds in up to 90%; mortality 8–22%
  • Treatment is two-pronged: (1) control bleeding (bypassing agents, porcine FVIII that the antibody doesn't recognize); (2) eradicate the inhibitor (immunosuppression)
High yield

Older adult + new extensive bruising + isolated ↑ PTT that doesn't correct + very low FVIII = acquired FVIII inhibitor. Liver disease would prolong the PT too.

Q3. Why does a "lupus anticoagulant" cause clots?
  • Heterogeneous autoantibodies against phospholipid-binding proteins (β2-glycoprotein I, prothrombin)
  • In the tube: they block tests that use a limited supply of phospholipid → ↑ aPTT (± PT)
  • In the body: prothrombotic → stroke, DVT/PE, recurrent miscarriage
  • Has nothing to do with lupus for most patients; it was first found in lupus patients
#ISTH criteria for lupus anticoagulant
1At least one abnormal screening test (PTT-LA, dRVVT)
2No correction on mixing
3Shortening with excess phospholipid
4Exclusion of a specific factor inhibitor
Trap

Prolonged clotting time ≠ bleeding risk. A surgeon sees a long PTT, but this patient needs clot prevention, not plasma.

Step 1

Antiphospholipid syndrome = clot or pregnancy event + persistent antibody (lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin, anti-β2GPI) on 2 tests ≥12 weeks apart → long-term warfarin. Can cause a false-positive VDRL.

MCQExam-style questions

Score: 0 / 0

1An 82-year-old man with no bleeding history has 3 weeks of extensive deep forearm bruises. Platelets 343,000; PT 11 s; PTT 83 s; mixing study 60 s; thrombin time normal. Most likely diagnosis?

New bleeding in an older adult + isolated ↑ PTT that doesn't correct → acquired hemophilia A. Liver disease prolongs the PT; heparin raises the TT.

2A 10-year-old boy has nosebleeds and gum bleeding; his brother is similar. Platelets 300,000 with normal morphology; PT and aPTT normal. Aggregation is normal to ristocetin but absent to thrombin. Defect?

Everything fails except ristocetin → Glanzmann thrombasthenia.

3A 43-year-old man has an ischemic stroke. PT 25 s, PTT 62 s, PTT mix 42 s (partial correction), dRVVT check/sure ratio 1.4, FVIII 85% with no inhibitor. Diagnosis?

Mix fails, excess phospholipid corrects, no specific factor inhibitor, and he clotted → lupus anticoagulant.

4A 24-year-old woman has easy bruising, 7-day heavy periods and childhood nosebleeds. PT 12 s, aPTT 31 s, platelets 275,000. Which workup is best?

Suspected vWD: order vWF antigen + activity, then aggregation. Normal screening labs never rule it out.

5A 45-year-old woman has headache and fever. Hb 6.7, platelets 5,000, creatinine 2.8, LDH 762, retics 6.3%, schistocytes on smear. Test most likely to yield the diagnosis?

TTP: ADAMTS13 activity <10% confirms. Start plasma exchange without waiting.

6A boy with factor IX activity of 0.5% asks how his bleeding will behave without treatment. Best answer?

<1% = severe → spontaneous hemarthroses and muscle bleeds.

7Which therapy has no role in hemophilia B?

DDAVP releases vWF and FVIII, not FIX.

8In TTP, the deficient enzyme normally does which of the following?

ADAMTS13 is a metalloprotease that trims vWF multimers.

9A patient with suspected TTP has platelets of 8,000. Which step should be avoided?

Platelets feed the microthrombi; avoid unless life-threatening bleeding.

10A 50-year-old's pre-op aPTT is 85 s and corrects on mixing. He had no bleeding after a tonsillectomy or dental extractions. Most likely?

Long aPTT that corrects with no bleeding → FXII. Lupus anticoagulant wouldn't correct.

11A woman has two second-trimester losses and a DVT. aPTT is prolonged and doesn't correct on mixing. Best next test?

Clots + pregnancy loss + non-correcting mix → antiphospholipid syndrome; confirm persistence at 12 weeks.

12An acquired FVIII inhibitor is confirmed. Why is porcine FVIII useful?

The autoantibody targets human FVIII; porcine FVIII escapes it while immunosuppression eradicates the inhibitor.
