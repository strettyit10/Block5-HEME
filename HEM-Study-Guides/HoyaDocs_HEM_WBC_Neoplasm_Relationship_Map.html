1 · The map

Two questions place every white cell disorder

Rows are the lineage (decided by flow cytometry markers). Columns are the tempo: mature cells growing slowly, mature cells growing fast, or blasts piling up behind a maturation block. Dashed chips sit in more than one box, usually because the disease changes over time. Select any disorder to see its essentials and everything it connects to; connected disorders light up on the map.

The two-question sort: (1) Which lineage? Myeloid (MPO, CD13, CD33, CD117) vs B (CD19, CD20) vs T (CD3) by flow cytometry. (2) How mature? ≥20% blasts (TdT+ or CD34+) = acute leukemia; blasts increased but <20% with dysplasia = MDS; mature cells = chronic leukemia, MPN or mature lymphoma.
Hodgkin lymphoma is graded by subtype, not by indolent vs aggressive biology. Its placement here follows stage at presentation and prognosis: lymphocyte-rich, nodular sclerosis and NLPHL usually present early with excellent outcomes; mixed cellularity and lymphocyte-depleted present later with B symptoms.

3 · Workup path

From "high WBC" or "big node" to a WHO diagnosis

The same routine runs through all four lectures: reactive or neoplastic, then lineage and maturity, then the specific entity, then the specimen and tests that prove it. Each step has the matching flowcharts from the lecture notes.

1 · Reactive or neoplastic?
Points to reactive
  • Fever, infection, steroids or epinephrine (demargination)
  • WBC usually <30k with bands, toxic granulation, Döhle bodies, high LAP
  • Tender, rapidly enlarging nodes in a febrile young patient
  • High platelets with low ferritin or high CRP; high Hct with high EPO
Points to neoplastic
  • WBC >50k with all stages and basophilia; splenomegaly; no fever
  • Blasts in the blood (never normal)
  • Painless, rubbery, persistent nodes (>2 cm, supraclavicular, >4–6 weeks)
  • Unexplained cytopenias with dysplasia; B symptoms
The A-B-C of marrow failure
  • Anemia → fatigue, pallor
  • Bleeding → petechiae (low platelets)
  • Contagion → infection (low neutrophils)
  • Seen in acute leukemia, MDS, hairy cell, marrow-involving lymphoma
↓
2 · Which lineage, and how mature?
Myeloid
  • MPO, CD13, CD33, CD117; Auer rods only here
  • ≥20% blasts → AML
  • <20% blasts + dysplasia + cytopenias → MDS
  • Mature cells, high counts, big spleen → MPN
B cell
  • CD19, CD20, CD22, CD79a; κ or λ restriction = clonal
  • TdT+, CD10+ blasts → B-ALL
  • Small mature cells → indolent lymphoma / CLL
  • Large cells in sheets → DLBCL or Burkitt
T cell · Hodgkin
  • CD2, CD3, CD5, CD7: TdT+ → T-ALL; CD4+ mature → MF/Sézary or ATLL
  • Few large cells in a reactive crowd: CD15+ CD30+ → classic Hodgkin; CD20+ popcorn → NLPHL
↓
3 · Name the entity
Myeloid
  • BCR-ABL + → CML
  • BCR-ABL −, JAK2/CALR/MPL: high Hct + low EPO → PV · platelets >600k (in-house) → ET · fibrosis + teardrops → PMF
  • AML: t(15;17) + DIC → APL · gum hypertrophy → monocytic · Down syndrome <5 → M7
Small B cells
  • CD5+: CD23+ → CLL · cyclin D1+ → mantle cell
  • CD10+, BCL2+ follicles → follicular
  • Neither: mucosal site → MALT · IgM spike → LPL · CD103 + huge spleen → hairy cell
Large B · T · Hodgkin
  • Starry sky, t(8;14), Ki-67 ≈100% → Burkitt; otherwise DLBCL
  • HTLV-1 + hypercalcemia → ATLL; skin rash + Pautrier → MF
  • Lacunar cells → NS · eosinophils + EBV → MC · HIV + few lymphocytes → LD
The big four lymphoma translocations all involve IGH on chromosome 14: t(8;14) MYC → Burkitt · t(14;18) BCL2 → follicular · t(11;14) cyclin D1 → mantle cell · t(11;18) API2-MALT1 → MALT (no response to H. pylori eradication).
↓
4 · Get the right specimen, then treat
Blood or marrow disease → marrow aspirate (morphology, flow, cytogenetics) + core biopsy (cellularity, fibrosis, paratrabecular aggregates). Mass → excisional node biopsy (architecture), not FNA. Then flow, IHC, cytogenetics/FISH and PCR/NGS → WHO diagnosis → staging (CT/PET, LDH) → therapy: TKIs for CML, phlebotomy + aspirin for PV, ATRA for APL, H. pylori eradication for gastric MALT, R-CHOP for DLBCL, ABVD for Hodgkin, LP and CNS prophylaxis for every ALL.
LDH and uric acid track tumor turnover. Burkitt and other fast tumors → tumor lysis syndrome with chemo (↑ K⁺, ↑ phosphate, ↑ uric acid, ↓ Ca²⁺, AKI): prevent with fluids plus allopurinol or rasburicase.

7 · Check yourself

Application questions on the relationships

Answer before you open each one.

A CML patient stable on imatinib for 5 years now has 30% blasts that are CD10+, CD19+ and TdT+. What happened, and why is a lymphoid outcome possible in a "myeloid" disease?

Lymphoid blast crisis (≈30% of blast crises; ≈60% are myeloid). The BCR-ABL translocation arose in a pluripotent stem cell above the myeloid/lymphoid split.

A 74-year-old has pancytopenia, MCV 108, normal B12 and folate, bilobed hypogranular neutrophils and 9% marrow blasts with monosomy 7. Eighteen months later blasts are 34%. Name both stages and the prognosis.

MDS → AML arising from MDS. Blasts <20% with dysplasia, then ≥20%. Monosomy 7 in an older patient = unfavorable, worse than de novo AML with a balanced translocation.

A 62-year-old with fever and pneumonia has WBC 28,000 with bands, toxic granulation and Döhle bodies. Another 59-year-old has WBC 180,000 with all stages, 8% basophils and a spleen 10 cm below the costal margin. Which single test separates them, and what does each show?

BCR-ABL (FISH/PCR): negative in the leukemoid reaction, positive in CML. The LAP score is high in the reactive patient and low in CML.

A 69-year-old with known CLL for 10 years develops rapid splenic enlargement, rising LDH and B symptoms. What happened, and which other indolent lymphomas do the same thing?

Richter transformation to DLBCL (≈10% of CLL). Follicular lymphoma (30–50%, often with MYC), MALT lymphoma and NLPHL can also transform to DLBCL.

A small-B-cell lymphoma is CD5+. Which two stains split the possibilities, and how do the two diseases differ in outcome?

CD23 (CLL+, mantle−) and cyclin D1 (mantle+, CLL−). CLL/SLL survives a median of 4–6 years and is often watched; mantle cell (t(11;14)) is the worst of the small-cell lymphomas at 3–5 years.

A 54-year-old woman has a gastric lymphoma with H. pylori and dense CD5−, CD10− small B cells invading glands. First treatment, and which genetic finding predicts it won't work?

MALT lymphoma → H. pylori eradication (antigen-driven, can regress). t(11;18) API2-MALT1 predicts resistance.

A 58-year-old Japanese man has hypercalcemia, lytic lesions, skin nodules and WBC 60,000 with clover-leaf lymphocytes. Which plasma cell disorder must you not jump to, and what is the cause instead?

Not multiple myeloma. CD4+ T cells with clover-leaf nuclei = ATLL from HTLV-1 (Tax). Both cause hypercalcemia with lytic lesions.

A 45-year-old woman treated at 17 with mantle radiation and ABVD for nodular sclerosis Hodgkin now has pancytopenia with dysplasia and −5/−7. Connect the two diseases.

Therapy-related MDS/AML: alkylating agents → −5/−7 after 5–10 years (topoisomerase II inhibitors → 11q23 after 1–3 years). It progresses to AML faster and has a poor prognosis. Other late effects: breast cancer, cardiac disease, pulmonary fibrosis.
