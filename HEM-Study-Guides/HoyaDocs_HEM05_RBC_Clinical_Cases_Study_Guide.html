In-house exam

This session is formative: expect the exam to give a short history and a lab panel and ask for the next test, the diagnosis, the mechanism behind an odd lab (e.g., high LDH in B12 deficiency), or the treatment and its duration.

LO 1Symptoms and history

Q1. What symptoms suggest anemia in these patients?
  • Fatigue for months
  • Exertional dyspnea: stopped exercising because she gets "winded"; can no longer walk from the Metro to the office without resting
  • Exam: pallor; tachycardia with exertion
Trap

Both patients have Hb 7.8 yet still walk around: slow-onset anemia is compensated. The speed of the drop, not the number, drives symptoms.

High yield
  • Dark urine → hemoglobinuria / bilirubin → hemolysis.
  • Rash or joint pain → autoimmune disease (lupus, RA) → autoimmune hemolytic anemia.
  • Pica / pagophagia (ice) → strongly suggests iron deficiency.
  • Numbness and tingling → B12 deficiency.
Memory tool

"Bleed, Eat, Take, Inherit, Cut, Show": Bleeding (menses, pregnancies, GI), Eating (diet, pica), Taking (medications), Inherited (family history), Cut (surgery), Show (other symptoms).

Analogy — timing as a clue

Anemia found before her first period points to something she was born with (hemoglobinopathy). Anemia only after menarche can still be iron deficiency, with or without an inherited trait.

ApplyLO 1 application questions

A1Why ask how many tampons a patient uses in 24 hours?
It turns "heavy" into a number and estimates how much blood (and iron) she loses each cycle.
A2Which single history item in Case 2 most changes the differential?
Gastric bypass surgery: it bypasses the duodenum (iron) and impairs absorption of food-bound B12.

ApplyLO 2 application questions

A1Microcytic anemia with RDW 13%, RBC count 5.6 million and normal ferritin. Top diagnosis?
Thalassemia trait (uniform small cells, high RBC count, normal iron).
A2Why is hemolysis unlikely in Case 2 despite high LDH and low haptoglobin?
Retics are low and bilirubin is normal; cells are dying inside the marrow (ineffective erythropoiesis), not in the circulation.

In-house exam

Know which iron test reflects stores (ferritin; TIBC indirectly) versus recent intake (plasma iron, transferrin saturation). Hemolysis tests (LDH, haptoglobin, bilirubin) are low-yield in Case 1 because nothing suggested hemolysis.

High yield — reading the CBC
  • Mild thrombocytosis is common in iron deficiency.
  • Microcytosis with a low RBC count → iron deficiency; normal/high RBC count → thalassemia.
  • High RDW in iron deficiency because cell size tracks recent iron supply; thal trait cells are uniformly small.
  • Low retics + low platelets + low WBC → production problem (Case 2).
Trap

Case 1's transferrin saturation is normal despite profound iron deficiency: saturation reflects recent intake. Trust the ferritin.

Q3. Why are LDH high and haptoglobin low in Case 2 if she isn't hemolyzing?

Ineffective erythropoiesis. B12 deficiency stops precursors from maturing; they die inside the marrow (intramedullary destruction) and release their contents → ↑ LDH, ↓ haptoglobin, and pancytopenia.

ApplyLO 3 application questions

A1A patient with rheumatoid arthritis flare has ferritin 180 and low serum iron. Does the ferritin rule out iron deficiency?
No. Ferritin is an acute phase reactant and can be falsely elevated by inflammation.
A2Why check creatinine in an anemia work-up?
Renal insufficiency → ↓ erythropoietin → hypoproliferative anemia.

  • Anisocytosis: variation in size

Q4. What would the marrow show?
  • Iron deficiency: absent iron stores (Prussian blue)
  • B12 deficiency: hypercellular, megaloblastic precursors in all lines, ↑ apoptosis (ineffective erythropoiesis)
  • Marrow biopsy is usually not needed when blood tests explain the anemia

ApplyLO 4 application questions

A1Smear shows two red cell populations, one small and pale and one large and oval. What combination?
Iron deficiency + B12 (or folate) deficiency: dimorphic picture, high RDW, MCV may be normal.

LO 5Treatment and follow-up

Q1. How should Case 1 be treated, and for how long?
  • Oral iron: ferrous sulfate 300 mg at least twice daily (transfusion and IV iron not needed for a stable patient absorbing normally)
  • Duration: until Hb and Hct are normal and ferritin >50 (or simply until ferritin >50)
  • If she also has α-thal trait, Hb, Hct and MCV may always stay slightly low, so don't chase a "normal" MCV
  • Monitoring: CBC and ferritin every 4–6 months while she menstruates, restarting iron before anemia returns; keep ferritin ≈50 or higher
In-house exam

Answer choices on duration: "until Hb/Hct normal" is wrong (stores not refilled); "until MCV normal" is wrong (may never normalize with α-thal trait). Correct: until ferritin >50.

Q2. How should Case 2 be treated, and for how long?
  • Both nutrients: either parenteral B12 + IV iron, or high-dose oral B12 on an empty stomach + parenteral iron
  • Why parenteral iron: bypass skips the duodenum, the main site of iron absorption
  • Why parenteral or high-dose B12: bypass impairs release and absorption of food-bound B12; high doses can be absorbed passively
  • Duration: B12 lifelong (anatomy won't change); iron by monitoring, replace when ferritin falls below 50
Trap

Giving only oral iron and B12 after a bypass fails: the absorption sites are bypassed. Giving iron alone would leave the neuropathy progressing.

Step 1

Post-bariatric deficiencies: iron, B12, folate, thiamine, vitamin D/calcium, copper. Copper deficiency can mimic B12 deficiency (myelopathy + anemia).

ApplyLO 5 application questions

A1Case 1's Hb normalizes after 8 weeks of oral iron; ferritin is 18. Stop?
No: continue until ferritin is above 50 to refill stores.
A2How long will Case 2 need B12?
Lifelong.

MCQExam-style questions

Score: 0 / 0

1A 30-year-old woman has 2 months of unsteady gait and numb legs. Eight years ago she had her terminal ileum resected for severe Crohn disease. She has mild spastic weakness and decreased pinprick, vibration and fine touch in all limbs. Deficiency of which is most likely?

Terminal ileum = B12–IF absorption site. B12 deficiency → peripheral neuropathy, ataxia, spasticity (subacute combined degeneration), cognitive and mood changes.

2A 16-year-old boy is evaluated for jaundice. Liver enzymes are normal, direct Coombs is negative, MCHC is increased, and RBC osmotic fragility is increased. Which cell is most likely on the smear?

Hereditary spherocytosis: ankyrin/spectrin defects, ↑ osmotic fragility, ↑ MCHC, DAT negative.

3A 46-year-old woman with advanced kidney disease from analgesic use has fatigue. Hb 8.8, MCV 82, ferritin 262, normal folate, transferrin saturation 22%, LDH 62. Most likely cause of her anemia?

Normocytic anemia + advanced kidney disease → ↓ EPO. Normal ferritin rules out iron deficiency; low LDH rules out hemolysis.

4An 18-year-old woman with sickle cell disease has right upper quadrant pain after a fatty meal; ultrasound shows gallstones. Underlying cause?

Chronic hemolysis → excess unconjugated bilirubin → pigment (bilirubin) stones.

5A 60-year-old man has 4 months of weakness, tingling hands and feet, irritability and forgetfulness; spasticity, hyperreflexia and absent vibration sense. Labs: megaloblastic anemia, antiparietal cell antibodies, ↑ MMA and ↑ homocysteine. Synthesis of which amino acid is impaired?

B12 is the cofactor for methionine synthase (homocysteine → methionine).

6A 20-year-old with heavy menses has Hb 7.8, MCV 62, RDW 19, iron 25, TIBC 371, ferritin 2. What is the best treatment?

Stable, absorbing normally → oral ferrous sulfate at least twice daily.

7For the patient in question 6, when should iron be stopped?

Treat until stores are refilled: ferritin >50. MCV may never normalize if she also has α-thal trait.

8Nine months after gastric bypass, a woman has pancytopenia, low retics, B12 112, LDH 535, haptoglobin 15 and normal bilirubin. Why are LDH and haptoglobin abnormal?

B12 deficiency → precursors die in the marrow (intramedullary destruction) → ↑ LDH, ↓ haptoglobin, with low retics and normal bilirubin.

9For the patient in question 8 (B12 and iron deficiency after bypass), which regimen is most appropriate?

Bypass impairs both absorptions → parenteral B12 (or high-dose oral on an empty stomach) + IV iron; B12 lifelong.

10A microcytic patient's Hb electrophoresis is normal. Which diagnosis is still possible?

α-thal trait has a normal electrophoresis in adults; β-thal trait shows ↑ HbA2.

11Which iron study best reflects total body iron stores?

Ferritin (indirect, but best). Plasma iron and saturation mainly reflect intake over the last 24–48 h.

12A young woman with anemia reports joint pains and a facial rash. Which type of anemia does this history raise?

Rash + arthralgia → lupus → warm AIHA.
